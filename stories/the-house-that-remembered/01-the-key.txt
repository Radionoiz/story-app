---
title: The Key
order: 1
---
The key arrived in a plain envelope three weeks after the funeral. There was no letter, only an address Lena knew from stories her mother had stopped telling.

The house stood at the end of a narrow road, its blue paint faded almost gray. When Lena fitted the key into the lock, something moved behind the upstairs curtains.

Inside, every clock showed a different hour.

She stepped into the parlor. The wallpaper brightened. A fire appeared in the cold grate, and two girls materialized on the rug, arguing over a wooden horse.

One of them was her mother.

Lena reached toward her, but the room blinked. The fire vanished. Dust settled through her open hand.

From somewhere upstairs came the sound of a door closing.
