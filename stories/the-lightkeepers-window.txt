---
title: The Lightkeeper's Window
tags: coastal, magical realism
date: 2026-07-18
excerpt: Every night, Mara lit the lamp for ships that no longer came. Then one evening, someone answered.
---
Every evening at dusk, Mara climbed the one hundred and seven iron steps to the lantern room. The sea below had forgotten the old shipping lanes, but she lit the lamp anyway.

Her father had taught her the ritual: trim the wick, polish the lens, open the western shutter. “A light is a promise,” he used to say. “It doesn't matter if anyone is there to see it.”

On the first night of autumn, a second light appeared beyond the black water.

It was too low to be a star and too steady to be a boat. Mara watched it until morning, when it vanished into the blue. The next night it returned. She covered her lamp with her hand, and the distant light went dark. She uncovered it. The light returned.

For seven nights they spoke this way: light and darkness, question and reply. On the eighth, Mara packed bread, a wool coat, and her father's brass compass. At dawn she pushed the little rowboat into the surf.

The compass pointed not north, but toward the fading glow.
